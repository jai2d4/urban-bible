# Episode 8 — "The Olive Leaf" (Genesis 8)

**Visual frame:** 2:3 cinematic. Keep people to backs, hands, silhouettes, and details; do not invent faces. No glow or spectacle—the weather and the wreckage do the work.

## Scene 1 — The water lets go

**INT. ARK — GRAY MORNING**

Rain has stopped. The hull still groans, but the endless pounding is gone. In the quiet, water drips from the rafters one drop at a time.

A row of tally marks runs along a beam: forty days of rain. Beside it, more marks—days adrift after the storm. The family has been shut in far longer than the storm lasted. Damp sleeves cling to wrists. Knees straighten slowly. One hand steadies a child who sways when the floor tilts.

Nohan moves among the animals, checking latches and water. His blue bandana is damp at his forehead; his flannel and tool belt hang within reach. His carpenter's hands, nicked and swollen, work by habit. A goat shifts away from his touch; he waits, then tries again. Nobody has enough room to sleep flat. Nobody complains.

The hull scrapes stone. Everything stops.

**EXT. MOUNTAIN RIDGE — LATER**

The ark has settled against a rocky shoulder above the city. Nohan eases open a shutter. Below, the flood has pulled back in broad, brown sheets. It leaves wreckage caught in fences and traffic lights, cars sideways in intersections, mud lines halfway up brick walls. A bus leans against a storefront. The street grid is still there; the life that filled it is not.

No sirens. No generators. Just wind moving a loose sign and water draining through a storm grate clogged with leaves.

Nohan watches the ridge beyond the rooftops. He waits.

## Scene 2 — The first flight

Nohan opens the ark window and releases a crow.

It circles the wreckage, landing on a snapped billboard frame, then lifting again. It finds places to perch among the debris and never comes back to the window.

After seven days, Nohan sends out a white pigeon.

It returns before dark, wings low with fatigue. Empty-beaked. Nohan gives it water from a shallow cup. One of the family’s hands rests beside the cup, not quite touching the bird.

Seven days pass. The mud below dries at the edges; the rooftops emerge one by one. Nohan sends the pigeon again.

At dusk it returns with a small olive leaf pinched in its beak. It came from an olive tree in a rooftop garden above the floodline. Nohan turns the leaf between two rough fingers. It is green. Not much, but it grew somewhere the water has left behind—a small thing, easy to miss in all that wreckage, and proof that something lived.

Nobody cheers. One person lets out a breath held too long. Nohan lays the leaf on the sill where the whole family can see it.

Seven more days. The pigeon goes out again.

It does not return.

Nohan keeps the window open through sunset. In the city below, water slips off a rooftop garden and runs down a drain. A bent streetlight stands over an empty avenue.

## Scene 3 — Dry ground

Time passes. The waterline drops below the rooflines, then the streets. The exposed ground changes from slick mud to cracked earth. The family peels back the ark’s covering and looks out at a washed-clean horizon.

The Voice reaches them without thunder.

**THE VOICE**
> Come out of the ark. Bring your family and every living thing with you. Let life fill the earth again.

Nohan steps down first. His boot presses into the ground. It holds.

He waits before taking the next step. His family follows—one hand gripping the doorway, another reaching back to help. Their legs tremble after months of narrow floors and careful steps. One person kneels and puts both palms on the dry earth. No one speaks.

Beyond them, the city is stripped to its bones: washed-out storefronts, mud-stained walls, tangled power lines, and whole blocks of silence. No familiar voices answer from the streets. Nohan looks over the empty rooftops, carrying the weight of all the people who are not coming back.

## Scene 4 — What he gives

On the solid concrete roof of a flood-stripped building above the ridge road, Nohan checks the surface for cracks. He chooses a sound place and builds a low altar from stones cleared out of a drain.

From the animals set apart for an offering, he chooses a goat. He does not make a show of it. The family turns their eyes away for the moment the life is taken. Nohan lays the offering on the stones and lights it. Smoke rises into a sky finally beginning to clear.

Over the silent blocks, the Voice answers. It does not erase the mud line, mend the roofs, or bring the lost back.

**THE VOICE**
> I will never again curse the ground this way or wipe out every living thing. As long as the earth remains, planting and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will not cease.

Wind pushes the smoke across the roof. Down on the block, a rain gutter releases its last trapped water. Nothing glows. Nothing is repaired. The promise is spoken over the damage that remains.

## Scene 5 — A marker in the sky

A final band of rain passes beyond the city. Sunlight reaches through the broken clouds and catches the rain over the ridge. A rainbow stretches above the rooftops—not a flash, not a sign painted in the air, just light and water held in the sky.

Nohan lifts his hand and points. His family gathers beside him. They look from the rainbow to the city below: the drowned blocks, the wet rooftops, the first green leaf.

The rainbow stays while the last rain drains off the streets.
