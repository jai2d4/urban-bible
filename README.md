# Urban Bible

Urban retelling of the entire Bible as modern-day street stories. Genesis through Revelation, told through the lens of present-day urban life — the same choices, the same consequences, the same Voice. Built for elementary-age viewers.

## Core Concept

- **One straight storyline.** Creation to Babel is humanity building and breaking. Abraham starts the repair. The promise runs through Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Egypt, Moses, the law, the kingdom, the prophets, exile, and the New Testament — birth, temptation, teachings, crucifixion, resurrection, Pentecost. Pentecost is Babel reversed: one Spirit, every language at once. Same Voice, same story, full circle.
- **Nothing new under the sun.** Present-day setting, not a specific decade. The stories aren't ancient — they're recurring. Same dynamics on the same blocks viewers walk past every day.
- **Cause-and-effect chain in every episode.** The choice, the physical toll, the spiritual weight — all visible in one body on one block. Gritty and real is the floor, not the ceiling.
- **The lesson is baked in, never preached.** Drugs, violence, disobedience — shown through physical fallout the viewer can see and connect to the biblical consequence. No sermon. The body keeps the receipt.
- **Realistic or nothing.** No fantasy elements, no zoo animals, no supernatural glow for its own sake. A typhoon, not water from below. Cane corsos, not lions. Kids clock fake fast.
- **The city reacts; it doesn't get replaced.** The setting is a real urban environment. Divine events happen *to* the city, not in a separate fantasy world.
- **Double-meaning titles.** Every episode title works as a street-level hook (sounds like a true-crime doc or Netflix drama row) AND carries the biblical weight. E.g., "The Door That Shut Itself" = the ark's door and every missed chance.

## Season 1 — Genesis (Episodes 1–11 in the arc)

### Episode 1 — "When the Lights Came On" (Genesis 1)
In the beginning, nothing but dark — no block, no lights, no sound. The Voice says let there be light and one sodium bulb flickers on in a window, then another, then a whole avenue. Day by day: the sky split, dry land pulling up out of the deep with green pushing through cracked pavement, sun and moon as clocks in the sky, water filling with life, animals, then man and woman made in that image. Day seven: the Voice rested — not tired, finished. First thing God ever called good was light.

### Episode 2 — "The First Rock" (Genesis 3)
The fall. A laundromat at 2 a.m. The woman is alone, folding somebody else's clothes. The voice comes through the dryer vent — not a person, just a tone that knows what she's thinking. It doesn't argue with the rule. It asks: did he really say you'd die? She starts explaining, and the more she explains, the smaller the rule sounds. The man walks in with headphones around his neck. She's already holding it — not an apple. A rock. He takes half without asking what it is. That's the bite.

**Physical fallout:** Heart slamming, pupils blown, everything sharp for a second — she sees the laundromat like she's never seen it. Then the crash: flat, gray, hands shaking, sweating through the hoodie. The thing she can't unsee is herself in the dryer-door reflection — not naked, just wrecked. The garden's closed to her now. The Voice's warning wasn't about dying that day literally — something in her died that day, and the body keeps the receipt. The lesson: one hit of crack can destroy your life. Disobedience has a physical toll you can see.

Shame isn't about being naked anymore. It's about what she let into her system.

### Episode 3 — "Bone of My Bone" (Genesis 2)
After creation, the Voice plants a garden in the east — one green lot in the middle of concrete, irrigated, alive. The man tends it. One rule: eat from any tree, just not the one in the center. The Voice sees him alone — that's not good. Animals come by: strays, pigeons, a mutt that won't leave. He names them all. None match. No companion. The Voice puts him under, pulls a rib, closes him back up. She just appears — standing in a doorway that wasn't empty a second ago. His reaction is the whole shot. Bone of my bone, flesh of my flesh. First love story, before anything goes wrong. They end naked and unashamed — zero guilt, zero fear. The tension sits under the surface because we know chapter three is coming.

Visually the softest episode: warm light through leaves, two people who don't know what shame is yet.

### Episode 4 — "The Shovel" (Genesis 4)
Cain and Abel. Two brothers, same block, same house. Cain works the land — landscaping, hauling, the visible work. Abel keeps livestock — pigeons, goats in a backyard pen, quiet work nobody claps for. Both bring an offering. The Voice looks at Abel's and not Cain's. Doesn't explain why. Cain's face drops. The Voice: why are you angry — if you do what's right you'll be accepted, but sin is crouching at the door like a dog that knows your name. Cain doesn't listen. Calls Abel out to the field. Not a knife fight. A shovel. One swing too hard.

The physical toll isn't on Abel — it's on Cain. His hands won't come clean. No matter how many times he scrubs, he still sees the dirt. The Voice doesn't send him to prison. He gets marked — a brand that says don't touch this man, he's mine to deal with. Cain wanders east, builds a city, names it after his son.

Lesson: unchecked anger doesn't just kill the other person, it kills the life you had.

### Episode 5 — "The List" (Genesis 5)
The genealogy. Adem had Sethon, Sethon had Enok, and the names stack like generations on a block — each one a little further from the garden. Most live their years, have kids, die. Standard. But Enok breaks the pattern — he doesn't die. He walks with the Voice so close that one day he's not there. No body, no grave. The block just stops seeing him. And Methu lives the longest of anybody — 969 years — and dies the same year the water comes. The list isn't decoration. It's a countdown hiding in plain sight, and Nohan's the last name before everything resets.

Visually: the family tree growing out of concrete, each name a face aging in real time. Enok's branch ends mid-air with no roots underneath.

### Episode 6 — "Two by Two" (Genesis 6)
The Voice tells Nohan the earth's done — violence on every corner, every thought bent wrong, only his family's clean. Build the ark. Not a yacht, a box. 300 cubits, rooms stacked, pitch sealing every seam. Nohan's a carpenter, not a preacher, so he just builds. Kids throw bottles. Old heads shake their heads. Rain? Ain't never rained like that.

The animals come anyway — but urban, not zoo. Cane corsos, pit bulls off their chains, a pair of Rottweilers trotting side by side down the median. Goats that used to roam the vacant lot show up at the ramp like they had an appointment. Even the strays that never trusted nobody line up. That's the part that scares the block more than the water.

### Episode 7 — "The Door That Shut Itself" (Genesis 7)
Seven days of silence after the last animal's in. Nohan's family's sealed inside. Outside, the block's still partying. Day seven ends, sky goes black in the afternoon. Then it starts — a typhoon. Category five, the kind that turns the sky green. The block's been watching the news for days: evacuation orders, gas lines, boarded windows. Nohan's still building. People think he's crazy right up until the outer bands start shredding roofs. Then the surge comes in from the coast — a wall of water moving inland like the ocean decided to relocate. Cars floating, houses peeling off foundations, the whole grid going dark.

The ark's the only thing on that block that doesn't move. Everything else is debris. 40 days. Nohan's family is just riding it, listening to the city drown underneath. No screaming you can make out — just the water taking everything.

Toll: you survive, but you hear the world you knew go under, and you can't do nothing about it.

### Episode 8 — "The Olive Leaf" (Genesis 8) — NEXT UP
The water going down. The ark settles on the mountains. Nohan opens the window, sends out a raven that circles and never comes back. Then a dove — first trip, nothing. Second trip, an olive leaf. Third trip, it doesn't come back. Dry ground. Nohan steps out, builds an altar, sacrifices. The Voice: never again will I curse the ground. Seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night won't stop. Then the rainbow — the first promise with a physical marker you can point to.

Visually the quietest, heaviest episode: the ark on a peak, water receding in sheets, rainbow breaking over everything that just drowned.

## Visual Style Guidelines

- **Aspect ratio:** 2:3 cinematic (poster-style, movie/print poster energy).
- **Reference style:** bold headlines, page numbers, territory maps, a sentence panel, a where-they-are-now row, court-record-style links. Dark multi-page infographic energy for case folders; cinematic for episode visuals.
- **No invented faces.** Real photographs of real named people from official releases or news only, when people are depicted.
- **The city reacts.** Streetlights, alleys, laundromats, backyards, vacant lots — real urban texture, not fantasy landscapes.
- **Physical fallout is always visible.** The body shows what the choice cost.
- **Closing card:** original biblical reference in small text, plus the urban name mapping when relevant.
