# Episode 10 — "The Map"

**Genesis 10**

## Story

At a crossroads cut between the river road and the old highway, Nohan's three sons stand with the land opening in every direction. Their faces stay out of frame. Japheth's line heads north and west. Ham's line moves south and east, toward the river valleys. Shem stays closest to the source. Their paths separate, and the space between them fills with names.

The episode becomes a family tree laid over a street map. Each branch runs out from the same household. One name becomes a cluster of names, then a route, then a region. The map grows like a territory chart, but the borders are not drawn by one marker: generations move, settle, and spread. Labels appear on each branch: Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech, and Tiras; Cush, Egypt, Put, and Canaan; Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, Aram, and Eber. Cush's line runs through Nimrod to Babel and other cities. Names that will matter later are already on the map.

The camera follows the tree as time passes. A small knot of marks becomes a neighborhood of marks; then the neighborhood reaches beyond the frame. The same family resemblance appears in outline and posture, but no individual descendant is invented or singled out. The movement is the story: people carrying names away from one another and making homes across the map.

Then the map slows at Shem's branch. The names narrow toward Eber, whose line leads toward the Hebrews and, later, Abraham. The line does not end there, but the camera holds on it long enough to show where the next story will come from. The nations have spread across the page; one family thread continues.

## Scenes

1. **The crossroads:** The three brothers stand at a road split, seen only from behind. Each takes a different route; the family tree begins at their feet.
2. **Generations:** Names multiply along branches as the years pass. Territory labels appear over the map, including Cush, Egypt, Put, and Canaan.
3. **The world spreads:** Roads, rivers, coastlines, and settlement marks extend outward. The tree and map become one picture, a record of where the families went.
4. **The line holds:** The visual noise falls away around Shem's branch. The names lead to Eber; the last frame stays on that line, poised for the story of Abraham.

## Physical fallout

The first crossing leaves three sets of footprints pointing away from one another. As generations spread, routes stretch farther and the original crossroads disappears beneath new roads and place names. The map holds the separation even after the people who made it are gone.
