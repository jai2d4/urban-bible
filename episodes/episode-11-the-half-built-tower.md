# Episode 11 — "The Half-Built Tower"

**Genesis 11**

## Story

The people move east and find a plain in Shinar. Flat land to the horizon. No stone to build with, so they make bricks: clay pressed into wooden molds, rows drying in the open air, then fired hard. The workers' palms get raw. Dust sticks to their sweat.

They share one language and one plan. Build a city. Build a tower high enough to reach the sky. Make a name for ourselves. Stay together so we won't be scattered across the earth. The first walls rise in straight lines. More hands join. Bricks move from one person to the next without a pause. The tower keeps climbing, and the crew works like one body.

The Voice comes down to see the city and tower, leaning over the work as if it were a model spread across a table. The people are one, and their words are shared; if they keep building this way, the Voice says, they will be able to carry out whatever they set their minds to. Then the Voice confuses their speech and scatters them across the earth.

It happens in the middle of work. One worker calls for a brick; the person holding it hears a sound that means nothing. Someone points up; the crew answers with words none of them can follow. Hands keep moving for a second after the instructions stop making sense. A brick hangs between two people, then drops into the dust.

There is no blast. No wall comes crashing down. The tower stays standing, half-built. The argument grows louder, then breaks into separate sounds. People try one another's words again and again; nobody can finish the job together. The cranes stay where they were left. Scaffolding rattles in the wind around empty floors.

The people leave in groups, carrying what they can. No one gets to finish the name they meant to make. The place is called Babel because there the shared speech was confused. The last sound is many languages across the same plain—each one clear to somebody, each one lost to somebody else.

## Scenes

1. **No stone:** A wide, flat plain. Workers shape and fire bricks because the land has no building stone. Clay stains their hands and sleeves.
2. **The plan:** A group gathers around a rough tower sketch: a city, a tower, a name, and a promise not to scatter.
3. **One crew:** Bricks pass in a smooth chain. The structure rises; the workers' backs bend and shoulders tire, but the shared rhythm holds.
4. **The Voice sees:** The view pulls back until the city looks small, like a model on a table. The Voice speaks; no face or glowing figure appears.
5. **Words break:** A sentence stops halfway through. A brick is left between two hands; a handshake misses. Frustration shows in strained voices, dusty throats, and hands that no longer know what to do next.
6. **Left unfinished:** The workers disperse. The crane is still up, the scaffolding is still wrapped around the tower, and no explosion or collapse hides what happened. The work simply stops.

## Physical fallout

The workers leave with cracked palms, sore backs, and throats scraped raw from trying to be understood. The tower remains as a physical record of a plan nobody can complete together. In its silence, the last frame holds on many mouths speaking at once, each voice distinct in the same frame.
