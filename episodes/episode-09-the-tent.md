# Episode 9 — "The Tent"

**Genesis 9**

## Story

The water has gone. The ark rests on high ground, but the old block is gone with it. Nohan brings a few seeds down to a reclaimed lot. Grass pushes through cracks in the concrete. Grapes climb a chain-link fence where somebody once hung laundry. He works the dirt with the same carpenter's hands that built a boat big enough to carry his family through the storm.

The Voice gives the family a new start: have children, fill the earth. Animals may be food now, but not with their blood—their life—still in them. Human life is different. Whoever takes a person's life must answer for that life before other people, because people bear God's image. The rules are plain. The people who have to live by them are still the same people who came through the flood.

Seasons pass. Nohan tends the vines, cuts back what tangles the fence, and gathers grapes into a bucket. He makes wine. One night he drinks past the point where he can stand. Inside his tent, he falls asleep uncovered.

Ham comes in, sees his father, and goes back out to tell his brothers. The tent flap falls behind him. Shem and Japheth take a cloth between them, turn their backs, and walk in backward. They cover Nohan without looking. Outside, the bucket is still beside the fence; a few grapes have rolled into the dirt.

When Nohan wakes, the light is already hard through the tent wall. He knows what happened. He speaks a curse over Canaan, Ham's son, and a blessing over Shem and Japheth. No one answers. The words settle over the family like another weight they will carry out of the tent.

The garden is still green. The rule about blood is still spoken. But the first new household has already found a way to wound one another. The reset did not change the heart.

## Scenes

1. **First green:** Nohan kneels in the reclaimed lot, working around broken glass and tough roots. New leaves catch on the old clothesline fence.
2. **The rules:** As the family works, the Voice lays out the new charge and the boundary around blood and human life. Nohan's hands pause over a cut grape; the others listen.
3. **Too much:** Later, a half-empty cup tips beside the tent. Nohan is asleep inside, covered only by shadow and the tent's edge; nothing is shown graphically.
4. **The choice:** Ham steps out and tells his brothers. Shem and Japheth take a blanket, face away, and cover their father walking backward.
5. **The words:** Morning. Nohan wakes with a dry mouth and grit on his cheek. The curse and blessing are spoken; the sons leave in different directions.

## Physical fallout

Nohan's hands, once steady on every plank, shake when he reaches for the cup. By morning his mouth is dry, his shoulders ache from sleeping on the ground, and his words make the tent feel smaller. Nobody is struck, but the family walks out carrying what was said.
